Small-Space Plyometric and Power Session

A simple backyard session for 14-year-old athletes to build elastic power, quick feet and stronger first-step explosiveness in a 10 x 10 yard space.

Space: 10 x 10 yards Time: 30-35 min Equipment: jump rope, line or tape, cones, light dumbbells
Session focus
  • Light contacts
  • Strong posture
  • Balanced landings
  • Fast effort, good control

Warm-Up Activity

Start with the rope. Move from easy rhythm to single-leg balance and coordination.

Warm-Up

Jump Rope Progression

  • Two feet: 30 seconds
  • Left leg: 20-30 seconds
  • Right leg: 20-30 seconds
  • Rest: 30 seconds
  • Total: 2 rounds

Coaching cues: Stay light on the feet, keep jumps small, relax the shoulders and find a smooth rhythm.

Why it helps: Prepares the feet, ankles and calves for faster contacts later in the session.

3

Plyometric Activities

These are the simple jumping activities for a small space. Focus on quality, not fatigue.

Plyometric 1

Pogo Jumps

  • Volume: 2 x 10-12 contacts
  • Goal: quick ankle bounce and short ground contact

Coaching cues: Stay tall, make quick contacts and bounce off the ground.

Simpler focus: Keep the jumps low and rhythmic instead of trying to jump high.

Plyometric 2

Snap Down to Stick

  • Volume: 2 x 5 reps
  • Goal: learn strong athletic landing positions

Coaching cues: Chest up, hips back, freeze the landing and control the body position.

Why it matters: Helps athletes absorb force safely before they move into more explosive work.

Plyometric 3

Lateral Line Hops

  • Volume: 2 x 10-15 seconds
  • Goal: quick side-to-side feet with quiet landings

Coaching cues: Stay light, move fast side to side and land softly.

Progression idea: Slightly increase speed only if the athlete keeps the movement tight and controlled.

Plyometric 4

Single-Leg Knee-Drive Hops

  • Volume: 2 x 5 each side
  • Goal: balance, single-leg force and knee-drive action

Coaching cues: Drive the knee high, keep balance on the support leg and stick the landing.

Why it matters: Connects balance and force production in a way that supports sprint mechanics.

4

Dumbbell Power Activities

These are the 3 attached dumbbell activities. Use light weight and keep the reps explosive and clean.

Power 1

Dumbbell Power Activity 1

  • Volume: 3 x 5 reps
  • Goal: strong posture and controlled force production

Coaching cues: Strong posture, move with control and power through the movement.

Power 2

Dumbbell Power Activity 2

  • Volume: 2-3 x 5 each side
  • Goal: stable base and explosive push from one leg

Coaching cues: Build a stable base, then explode up. Land softly and keep the torso quiet.

Power 3

Dumbbell Power Activity 3

  • Volume: 2-3 x 6 contacts
  • Goal: drive through the floor and finish balanced

Coaching cues: Drive through, stay controlled and finish balanced on every rep.

Coaching Rules

These rules keep the session safe and effective for younger athletes.

Quality firstStop the set if posture, rhythm or landing quality drops.
Jump low, not sloppyFast contacts matter more than big jumps.
Land softlyQuiet feet usually mean better control.
Use light dumbbellsPower work should still look quick and athletic.
Rest when neededTake 30-45 seconds between harder explosive sets.
Stay in the small spaceEverything in this page is designed for a 10 x 10 yard area.
